Introduction to Large Language Models

Understanding the fundamentals of LLMs and their revolutionary impact on AI

What are Large Language Models?

Large Language Models (LLMs) are AI systems trained on vast amounts of text data to understand and generate human-like text. They're the technology behind tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and many other AI assistants.

Key Characteristics

  • Scale: Billions or trillions of parameters that capture language patterns
  • Training Data: Massive text corpora from books, websites, code, and more
  • Capabilities: Text generation, translation, summarization, coding, and reasoning
  • Architecture: Based on the revolutionary Transformer model

The Transformer Architecture

The revolutionary architecture that changed natural language processing

Understanding Transformers

The Transformer, introduced in the 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need," revolutionized natural language processing. It's the foundation of all modern LLMs including GPT, BERT, and Claude.

Key Innovation: Unlike previous models that processed text sequentially, Transformers can process entire sequences in parallel, making them much faster and more effective at capturing long-range dependencies.

Why Transformers are Powerful

  • Parallelization: Process entire sequences at once, not word-by-word
  • Long-range dependencies: Capture relationships between distant words
  • Scalability: Performance improves with more data and parameters
  • Transfer learning: Pre-trained models can be fine-tuned for specific tasks

The Attention Mechanism

Understanding the core innovation that powers Transformers

What is Attention?

Attention is the mechanism that allows models to focus on relevant parts of the input when processing each word. It's like highlighting important words in a sentence to understand its meaning.

Types of Attention

Self-Attention

Each word attends to all other words in the sequence

Multi-Head Attention

Multiple attention mechanisms run in parallel

Cross-Attention

Attending between encoder and decoder sequences

How Attention Works (Simplified)

# For each word in the sentence: # 1. Create Query (what am I looking for?) # 2. Create Keys (what do I contain?) # 3. Create Values (what information do I have?) # Calculate attention scores scores = Query × Keys^T / sqrt(d_k) attention_weights = softmax(scores) # Get context-aware representation output = attention_weights × Values
The Power of Attention: By learning which words to focus on, models can understand complex relationships like pronouns referring to entities, or verbs relating to their subjects, even when they're far apart in the sentence.

Pre-training: Foundation Learning

Phase 1 - Where LLMs learn the fundamentals of language

What is Pre-training?

Pre-training is an unsupervised learning process where the model learns patterns, structures, and knowledge from vast amounts of text data without explicit labels. This is where the model builds its foundational understanding of language.

The Pre-training Objective

Most modern LLMs use next-token prediction (also called autoregressive or causal language modeling):

Given: "The cat sat on the" Predict: "mat" Given: "Machine learning is a subset of" Predict: "artificial" (then "intelligence")

The model learns to predict the probability distribution over all possible next tokens, given the preceding context.

Post-training: Alignment & Specialization

Phase 2 - Making models helpful, harmless, and honest

What is Post-training?

After pre-training, models undergo post-training to become helpful assistants, specialized tools, or domain experts. This phase makes models safe, aligned with human values, and optimized for following instructions.

Why It's Necessary: Pre-trained models can complete text but aren't optimized for following instructions, being helpful, or avoiding harmful outputs. Post-training aligns models with human preferences.

The Post-training Pipeline

1. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The model is trained on high-quality human-written examples of desired behavior.

Example Training Data:

User: "What is photosynthesis?" Assistant: "Photosynthesis is the process by which plants convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It occurs in chloroplasts and requires water, CO2, and sunlight, producing oxygen as a byproduct."

Thousands of such examples teach the model to be informative and helpful.

2. Reward Modeling

Human raters compare multiple model outputs to teach what "good" responses look like.

How it works:

  1. Model generates multiple responses to the same prompt
  2. Human raters rank responses from best to worst
  3. A reward model learns to predict human preferences
  4. This reward model guides further training

3. RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)

The model is fine-tuned using the reward model through reinforcement learning, optimizing for outputs humans prefer.

4. Additional Alignment Techniques

  • Constitutional AI (CAI): Models trained with explicit principles and self-critique
  • Direct Preference Optimization (DPO): Simpler alternative to RLHF
  • Red-teaming: Adversarial testing to find failure modes

Types of Fine-tuning

Full Fine-tuning

Update all parameters. Most flexible but expensive.

LoRA

Train small adapter layers. Much more efficient.

Prompt Tuning

Only optimize input prompts. Minimal updates.

Instruction Tuning

Teach models to follow diverse instructions.

Essential Concepts

Key concepts you need to understand about LLMs

Tokens & Tokenization

Tokens are the basic units that LLMs process. A token can be a word, part of a word, or even a character. Understanding tokenization is crucial for working with LLMs.

Example: "Understanding" might be split into ["Under", "stand", "ing"]

Embeddings

Words are converted into numerical vectors (embeddings) that capture their meaning. Similar words have similar vectors in this high-dimensional space.

Famous Example: "king" - "man" + "woman" ≈ "queen"

This shows how embeddings capture semantic relationships!

Context Window

The maximum amount of text the model can consider at once. This is a hard limit that affects what the model can "remember."

GPT-3

4K tokens
~3,000 words

GPT-4

32K-128K tokens
~24K-96K words

Claude

200K tokens
~150K words

Temperature & Sampling

Temperature controls randomness in text generation:

  • Low (0.1-0.5): More focused and deterministic - good for factual tasks
  • Medium (0.6-0.8): Balanced creativity and coherence
  • High (0.9-1.5): More creative and diverse - good for brainstorming
# Temperature affects probability distribution probabilities = softmax(logits / temperature) # Low temperature: probabilities become more peaked # High temperature: probabilities become more uniform
