What are Large Language Models (LLMs)?

Large Language Models are AI systems trained on vast amounts of text data to understand and generate human-like text. They're the technology behind tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and many other AI assistants.

Key Characteristics:

  • Scale: Billions or trillions of parameters
  • Training Data: Massive text corpora from books, websites, and more
  • Capabilities: Text generation, translation, summarization, coding, and reasoning
  • Architecture: Based on the Transformer model

Phase 1: Pre-training - Foundation Learning

Pre-training is where LLMs develop their foundational understanding of language. This phase requires enormous computational resources and massive datasets.

What is Pre-training?

Pre-training is an unsupervised learning process where the model learns patterns, structures, and knowledge from vast amounts of text data without explicit labels.

The Pre-training Objective

Most modern LLMs use next-token prediction (also called autoregressive or causal language modeling):

Given: "The cat sat on the" Predict: "mat" Given: "Machine learning is a subset of" Predict: "artificial" (then "intelligence")

The model learns to predict the probability distribution over all possible next tokens, given the preceding context.

Phase 2: Post-training - Specialization & Alignment

After pre-training, models undergo post-training to become helpful assistants, specialized tools, or domain experts. This phase makes models safe and useful.

Why Post-training is Necessary

Pre-trained models can complete text but aren't optimized for following instructions, being helpful, or avoiding harmful outputs. Post-training aligns models with human preferences.

1. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The model is trained on high-quality human-written examples of desired behavior.

Example Training Data:

User: "What is photosynthesis?" Assistant: "Photosynthesis is the process by which plants convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It occurs in chloroplasts and requires water, CO2, and sunlight, producing oxygen as a byproduct."

Thousands of such examples teach the model to be informative and helpful.

2. Reward Modeling

Human raters compare multiple model outputs to teach what "good" responses look like.

How it works:

  1. Model generates multiple responses to the same prompt
  2. Human raters rank responses from best to worst
  3. A reward model learns to predict human preferences
  4. This reward model guides further training

3. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)

The model is fine-tuned using the reward model through reinforcement learning, optimizing for outputs humans prefer.

4. Additional Alignment Techniques

Constitutional AI (CAI): Models are trained with explicit principles and can self-critique

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO): Simpler alternative to RLHF, directly optimizing preferences

Red-teaming: Adversarial testing to find and fix failure modes

Types of Fine-tuning

Full Fine-tuning

Update all model parameters. Expensive but most flexible.

LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)

Train small adapter layers. Much more efficient.

Prompt Tuning

Only optimize input prompts. Minimal parameter updates.

Instruction Tuning

Teach models to follow diverse instructions.

Safety and Alignment Challenges

  • Truthfulness: Ensuring models provide accurate information
  • Harmlessness: Preventing generation of harmful content
  • Bias Mitigation: Reducing unfair biases in outputs
  • Robustness: Handling adversarial inputs and edge cases
  • Value Alignment: Aligning AI behavior with human values

Essential Concepts in Generative AI

Tokens & Tokenization

Tokens are the basic units that LLMs process. A token can be a word, part of a word, or even a character.

Example: "Understanding" might be split into ["Under", "stand", "ing"]

Embeddings

Words are converted into numerical vectors (embeddings) that capture their meaning. Similar words have similar vectors.

Example: "king" - "man" + "woman" ≈ "queen"

Context Window

The maximum amount of text the model can consider at once. Modern models have context windows ranging from 4K to 200K+ tokens.

Temperature & Sampling

Temperature controls randomness in generation:

  • Low (0.1-0.5): More focused and deterministic
  • High (0.7-1.0): More creative and diverse
